# CLD-0107: SharePoint Mass Deletion Investigation

## Original Ticket

<img width="1915" height="925" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ac59435a-3ad1-4200-87ad-8020f0445202" />

**Short Description:**

Mass file deletion activity detected in SharePoint

**Description:**

Rule **"Mass deletion in SharePoint"** fired.

More than 500 files were deleted within a short period.

The activity originated from **olivia.ward@cloudora.io**.

The deletions occurred during a single session and exceeded the alert threshold for bulk file removal.

Evidence indicated a possible destructive action against SharePoint data.

---

## Investigation Strategy

Before beginning the investigation, I considered two possible explanations.

**Malicious hypothesis:**

A compromised account was intentionally deleting files from SharePoint to destroy or disrupt organizational data.

**Benign hypothesis:**

The activity was part of a legitimate administrative or automated process.

---

## Evidence Reviewed
<img width="868" height="793" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/bdef11e7-03e7-477f-bb56-46ba8fde846d" />

The first step was reviewing the SharePoint activity associated with **olivia.ward@cloudora.io**.

I examined the deletion timeline and compared it against the available operational documentation.

The shift handover identified a scheduled automation task responsible for removing outdated project files.

The timing of the alert aligned with the documented automation window.

I then compared the deletion events against the maintenance documentation.

The activity matched the expected behavior of the approved automation process.


---

## Investigation Process

My initial concern was the unusually high number of deleted files.

Mass deletion events can indicate account compromise, insider threats, ransomware activity, or attempts to destroy evidence.

However, several indicators supported the benign explanation.

The deletions occurred during the approved maintenance window.

The activity followed a predictable pattern instead of targeting specific files or directories.

There was no evidence of suspicious authentication activity.

There were no indicators of privilege escalation, unauthorized access, or abnormal user behavior.

The handover documentation confirmed that the deletions were generated by an automated cleanup process.

At that point, I ruled out the malicious hypothesis.

---

## Verdict

**False Positive**

The alert was triggered by an approved automated SharePoint cleanup process.

---

## Severity

### Informational – Expected and authorized automated activity.

**Why this severity was assigned:**

The alert was initially classified as High severity because of the large number of deleted files.

After reviewing the available evidence and validating the maintenance activity, the severity was reduced to Informational.

---

## Response Actions

- Closed the alert.
- Referenced the approved automation process.
- Documented the maintenance activity.
- Recommended excluding approved cleanup jobs from future alerting.

---

## Justification

The source, timing, and deletion pattern matched the documented SharePoint cleanup automation.

No evidence suggested malicious activity or account compromise.

---

## MITRE ATT&CK

**None.**

The activity was determined to be legitimate and did not require MITRE ATT&CK mapping.
