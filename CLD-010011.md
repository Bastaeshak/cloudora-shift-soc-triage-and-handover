# CLD-0111: Suspicious Supplier Invoice Email Investigation

## Original Ticket

**Short Description:**

User-reported suspicious supplier invoice email.

**Description:**

lucas.ford@cloudora.io (Accounts Payable) reported an email received at 08:19 UTC from `billing@meridianoffice.example` ("Meridian Office Supplies Ltd") with the subject **"Overdue invoice INV-2214, second reminder."**

The email contained an attachment named **INV-2214.pdf**.

Reporter note:

*"Not sure we use this supplier. Have not opened the attachment. Or I do not think I did."*

Evidence: `evidence/emails/02_reported_supplier_invoice.eml`

---

## Investigation Strategy

Before beginning the investigation, I considered two possible explanations.

**Malicious hypothesis:**

The email represented a phishing campaign that used a fraudulent invoice attachment to deliver malware or steal credentials.

**Benign hypothesis:**

The message was a legitimate invoice from a real supplier that was unfamiliar to the recipient.

---

## Evidence Reviewed
<img width="1919" height="934" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/928c13af-37f1-4f8e-a2a0-39da410289e2" />

I began by reviewing the email headers.

The message passed **SPF** validation.

However, **DKIM wasn't configured**, and **DMARC wasn't configured**.

Although SPF validation was successful, authentication alone wasn't enough to classify the message as legitimate.

I then reviewed the sender information.

The domain was unfamiliar to the user, and I couldn't confirm an existing business relationship between Cloudora and **Meridian Office Supplies Ltd**.

I also reviewed the message body.

The email created urgency by referring to an overdue invoice and potential late payment charges.

An attachment named **INV-2214.pdf** was included.

However, the attachment wasn't available in the training environment and couldn't be detonated or analyzed.

---

## Investigation Process

My first objective was determining whether the message could be confidently classified as either legitimate or malicious.

Several characteristics increased the risk score.

The message used an unfamiliar sender.

It contained an invoice attachment.

It requested payment.

It introduced a sense of urgency.

At the same time, several pieces of evidence prevented me from classifying the email as confirmed phishing.

SPF validation succeeded.

The attachment wasn't available for analysis.

I couldn't verify the supplier relationship.

I couldn't perform malware analysis on the PDF.

I couldn't determine whether the attachment contained malicious code.

Because the most important evidence wasn't available, I couldn't reach a defensible conclusion.

---

## Verdict

**Insufficient Data**

Additional evidence was required before the email could be classified as either legitimate or malicious.

---

## Severity

### Sev 3 – Suspicious activity requiring additional investigation.

**Why this severity was assigned:**

The email contained several phishing indicators.

However, the investigation couldn't confirm malicious intent because the attachment wasn't available for analysis.

---

## Response Actions

- Escalated the investigation.
- Requested the original PDF attachment.
- Requested attachment detonation results.
- Requested antivirus scan results.
- Requested verification from Accounts Payable regarding the supplier relationship.

---

## Justification

The available evidence suggested that the message was suspicious.

However, the absence of the original attachment prevented malware analysis.

Without the attachment, the investigation couldn't determine whether the email represented a phishing campaign, a malware delivery attempt, or a legitimate business communication.

---

## MITRE ATT&CK

No MITRE ATT&CK mapping was assigned.

The available evidence didn't support a confirmed attack technique.
