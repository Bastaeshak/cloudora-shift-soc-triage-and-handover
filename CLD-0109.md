# CLD-0109: Repeated Outbound Connections Investigation

## Original Ticket

**Short Description:**

Repeated outbound connections to an unclassified destination detected across multiple endpoints.

**Description:**

Rule **"Periodic outbound connections to uncategorized destination"** fired.

Since approximately **07:40 UTC**, three internal hosts:

- **LDN-WS-104 (aria.reid)**
- **MAN-LT-033 (finn.gale)**
- **AUS-WS-208 (dean.page)**

made outbound **HTTPS** connections to **198.18.207.66** approximately every **60 seconds**.

The destination wasn't categorized by the firewall threat feed, and the associated domain registration was only **9 days old**.

The affected hosts were located in **London, Manchester, and Austin**.

---

## Investigation Strategy

Before beginning the investigation, I considered two possible explanations.

**Malicious hypothesis:**

Multiple endpoints had been compromised and were communicating with a command-and-control server.

**Benign hypothesis:**

The connections were generated by a legitimate application, update service, or another authorized process.

---

## Evidence Reviewed

The first step was determining whether the activity represented an account compromise or an endpoint-related event.

I reviewed the available authentication data associated with the affected users and compared it against their normal activity.

Sign-in activity didn't reveal any evidence of suspicious authentication events, impossible travel, or unauthorized access.

Routine Microsoft 365 activity was observed across the affected accounts.

I then compared the three affected systems.

The most important observation was that the behavior was identical across all three hosts.

Each system connected to the same destination.

Each system used the same protocol.

Each system established connections at nearly identical intervals.

This pattern suggested an automated process rather than user-driven activity.


---

## Investigation Process

My initial concern was account compromise.

However, after reviewing the authentication activity, I found no evidence supporting unauthorized account access.

No suspicious sign-ins were identified.

No suspicious audit events were identified.

Because the affected systems were located in three different geographic regions, I shifted the investigation from identity analysis to endpoint analysis.

The repeated connections occurring every 60 seconds suggested beaconing behavior.

Beaconing is commonly associated with malware communicating with a command-and-control server.

The age of the destination domain increased my concern because recently registered domains are frequently associated with malicious infrastructure.

However, the available evidence didn't identify the process responsible for generating the network traffic.

Without endpoint telemetry, I couldn't determine whether the connections originated from malware, a legitimate application, or a software update process.

At that point, additional investigation was required.

---

## Verdict

**Escalate**

---

## Severity

### Sev 2 – Confirmed suspicious network activity requiring immediate investigation.

**Why this severity was assigned:**

Three independent systems established periodic outbound connections to the same unclassified destination.

The behavior was consistent with automated beaconing activity.

However, additional endpoint evidence was required to determine the underlying cause.

---

## Response Actions

- Escalated the incident for additional investigation.
- Recommended collecting endpoint telemetry from all three systems.
- Recommended identifying the parent process responsible for the network connections.
- Recommended collecting process, DNS, and network artifacts.

---

## Justification

The investigation didn't support an account-compromise scenario.

The evidence suggested that the activity originated from the endpoints themselves.

The synchronized communication pattern across multiple systems, combined with the newly registered destination, justified escalation.
