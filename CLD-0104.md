# CLD-0104: MFA Failures and New Device Registration Investigation

**Short Description:**

MFA failures followed by a successful sign-in and new device registration for nina.cole

**Description:**

Rule **"MFA failures then success with new device registration"** fired.

Five MFA denials occurred between **06:31 and 06:47 UTC**.

A successful sign-in occurred at **06:52 UTC**.

A new authenticator device was registered at **06:50 UTC**.

All activity originated from Nina Cole's usual Manchester office IP address.

A helpdesk ticket (**HD-4471**) was opened at **07:05 UTC**. Evidence: `cloudora_signin_slice.csv`. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

## Investigation Strategy

Before beginning the investigation, I considered two possible explanations.

**Malicious hypothesis:**

An attacker had already obtained Nina Cole's password and was attempting an **MFA fatigue attack** by repeatedly sending MFA prompts until one was approved.

The newly registered authenticator device could have represented an attempt to establish persistence, which closely matched the **CLD-0001 attack pattern** previously observed at Cloudora.

**Benign hypothesis:**

Nina Cole was replacing her phone and encountered problems while re-enrolling her MFA device. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

## Evidence Reviewed

I reviewed `cloudora_signins_.csv` and filtered all authentication activity associated with **nina.cole**.

The sign-in logs showed:

- Five MFA denials between **06:31 and 06:47 UTC**
- A successful sign-in at **06:52 UTC**
- All events originating from **198.51.100.22**
- All events occurring from a **Windows 10** device using **Chrome**
- All activity occurring during Nina Cole's normal morning work schedule. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

I then reviewed the helpdesk system and identified **HD-4471**, which had been opened by Nina Cole herself at **07:05 UTC**.

The helpdesk ticket specifically requested assistance with **re-enrolling MFA on a new phone**.

Finally, I compared the authenticator registration event against the authentication timeline.

The new device registration occurred at **06:50 UTC**, immediately before the successful sign-in at **06:52 UTC**, which was consistent with a successful MFA re-enrollment. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

---

## Investigation Process

My first objective was determining whether the activity matched an MFA fatigue attack.

Because Cloudora had previously experienced a compromise involving MFA persistence, I treated the new device registration as a potentially high-risk event.

However, one detail immediately stood out.

Every authentication event, including the failed MFA attempts, originated from Nina Cole's **known office IP address**, her **usual Windows 10 device**, and her **normal browser**.

An MFA fatigue attack typically involves an attacker authenticating from an unfamiliar location while the legitimate user repeatedly denies authentication requests.

That pattern was completely absent.

The helpdesk ticket became the deciding factor.

The timing of **HD-4471**, combined with the device registration event, supported the explanation that Nina Cole was replacing her phone and encountered difficulties while reconfiguring MFA.

At that point, I ruled out the malicious hypothesis.

---

## Verdict

**False Positive**

The alert was caused by user error during MFA re-enrollment while replacing a mobile device. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

## Severity

### Informational – Expected and authorized user activity.

**Why this severity was assigned:**

The alert was originally classified as **Medium severity**.

After reviewing the authentication logs and helpdesk ticket, the activity was reclassified as **Informational** because all evidence supported legitimate user activity. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

---

## Response Actions

- Closed the alert.
- Referenced helpdesk ticket **HD-4471**.
- Determined that no password reset was necessary.
- Recommended automatically annotating MFA re-enrollment alerts with active helpdesk tickets to reduce future false positives. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

---

## Justification

An MFA fatigue attack requires evidence of an attacker authenticating from an unfamiliar device or location.

Every authentication event in this investigation originated from Nina Cole's known workstation and office location.

The matching helpdesk request confirmed that the user was replacing her phone and re-enrolling MFA before the SOC investigation even began. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

---

## MITRE ATT&CK

**None.**

The activity was determined to be legitimate and did not require MITRE ATT&CK mapping. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}
