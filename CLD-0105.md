# CLD-0105: Payroll Email Investigation

## Original Ticket

**Short Description:**

User-reported suspicious email: 'Your August payslip is ready'

**Description:**

james.holt@cloudora.io reported an email received at 07:45 UTC from `payroll@cloudora.io` with the subject **"Your August payslip is ready."**

The message contained a link to a payslip portal.

Reporter note:

*"After last week I am not clicking anything from payroll. Please check."*

Per post-CLD-0002 policy, every user-reported email must be triaged.

Evidence:

`evidence/emails/01_reported_payslip_notification.eml`

---

## Investigation Strategy

Before beginning the investigation, I considered two possible explanations.

**Malicious hypothesis:**

A second payroll-themed phishing campaign was targeting Cloudora employees.

**Benign hypothesis:**

The email was the legitimate monthly payroll notification, and the user reported it because of the recent phishing campaign.

---

## Evidence Reviewed
<img width="1396" height="663" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9677b5a8-b3b9-413f-befb-a270062041af" />

The first step was opening the `.eml` file in a plain text editor and reviewing the email headers.

I examined the **From** field and verified that the message originated from `payroll@cloudora.io`, which matched Cloudora's legitimate domain.

I then reviewed the **Authentication-Results** header.

SPF, DKIM, and DMARC all passed.

More importantly, all three authentication mechanisms were aligned with `cloudora.io`.

After the previous payroll phishing campaign, I knew that passing authentication by itself wasn't enough.

The important question wasn't whether authentication passed.

The important question was **which domain passed authentication.**

---

## Investigation Process

My next step was reviewing the **Received** chain.

I analyzed the email path from the bottom up to determine where the message originated.

The email was generated internally by `myhr-notify.cloudora.io` (`203.0.113.8`) and remained entirely within Cloudora's infrastructure.

A spoofed message would have originated from an external source.

I then reviewed the message body.

The email linked only to `myhr.cloudora.io`, which is the legitimate employee payroll portal.

The message did not request credentials.

It did not request banking information.

It did not create urgency through countdown timers, payment deadlines, or account suspension warnings.

The email also explicitly stated that payroll would never ask employees for passwords or banking information through email.

At that point, I ruled out the phishing hypothesis.

---

## Verdict

**False Positive**

The message was a legitimate internal payroll notification.

---

## Severity

### Informational — Legitimate internal communication.

**Why this severity was assigned:**

The alert was originally classified as **Medium severity** because company policy requires every reported email to be investigated.

After completing the header analysis, the email was reclassified as **Informational** because all evidence confirmed that the message originated from legitimate Cloudora infrastructure.

---

## Response Actions

- Closed the alert.
- Replied to James Holt.
- Confirmed that the email was legitimate.
- Encouraged the user to continue reporting suspicious emails.

---

## Justification

The message passed SPF, DKIM, and DMARC validation while remaining aligned with the legitimate `cloudora.io` domain.

The email originated from Cloudora's internal infrastructure and never left the environment.

The message body contained no phishing indicators, no credential requests, and no attempts to collect sensitive information.

---

## MITRE ATT&CK

**None.**

The email was determined to be legitimate internal communication.
