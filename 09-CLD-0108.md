# CLD-0108: Unfamiliar Sign-In Properties Investigation

## Original Ticket

<img width="1915" height="930" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9d5bb4d2-8350-4d80-b763-95a9450f8741" />


**Short Description:**

Unfamiliar sign-in properties detected for gwen.muir@cloudora.io

**Description:**

Rule **"First-time sign-in properties"** fired.

A successful sign-in for **gwen.muir@cloudora.io** occurred at **03:54 UTC** from **198.18.163.41**.

The sign-in originated from **Lagos, Nigeria**.

The session used **IMAP4**, **Windows 10**, and **Firefox**.

The alert was triggered because these properties had not previously been observed for this account.

Evidence: `evidence/cloudora_signin_slice.csv`.

---

## Investigation Strategy

Before beginning the investigation, I considered two possible explanations.

**Malicious hypothesis:**

An attacker obtained valid credentials and successfully authenticated to the account.

**Benign hypothesis:**

The user legitimately accessed the account from a new location, device, or application, causing the alert to trigger.

---

## Evidence Reviewed

The first step was reviewing the sign-in activity associated with **gwen.muir@cloudora.io**.

I established a baseline by reviewing previous authentication events and comparing them with the alert.

The baseline showed that the user typically authenticated from the United Kingdom using familiar devices, browsers, and Microsoft 365 applications.

I then reviewed the alerting event and confirmed that the authentication originated from **Lagos, Nigeria** and used **IMAP4**, which differed from the established baseline.

<img width="1381" height="719" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/62b8cda5-4a02-4eb4-a3f6-d4093d5a4841" />


I used **KQL queries** to review authentication activity and determine whether the event represented an isolated incident or part of a larger pattern.

I also reviewed available audit logs to identify any mailbox modifications, permission changes, inbox rules, MFA changes, or other administrative activity.

The audit review didn't identify any suspicious account modifications.

KQL query used:

CloudoraSignIn_CL
| where UserPrincipalName == "gwen.muir@cloudora.io"
| summarize
    SignIns=count(),
    FirstSeen=min(TimeGenerated),
    LastSeen=max(TimeGenerated)
    by IPAddress, City, Country, DeviceOS, Browser, AppDisplayName
| order by SignIns desc
---

## Investigation Process

Because the authentication was successful, my first concern was account compromise.

However, there were several factors that prevented me from immediately classifying the event as a confirmed compromise.

The account had previously been targeted during the phishing campaign investigated in an earlier incident.

However, available evidence indicated that the user viewed the phishing email but didn't interact with the malicious link.

I also identified previous password-spraying attempts against the account.

Those attempts failed and didn't provide evidence that the attacker successfully obtained valid credentials.

The successful IMAP4 authentication remained the most concerning indicator because it represented a significant deviation from the user's normal behavior.

However, I couldn't identify any follow-up activity that would confirm malicious access.

The audit logs didn't show mailbox changes.

The investigation didn't identify any suspicious configuration changes.

No additional malicious activity was identified after the sign-in.

Because the available evidence couldn't confirm account compromise, additional investigation was required.


---

## Verdict

**Escalate**

The authentication event was determined to be suspicious and required escalation for additional investigation.

---

## Severity

### Sev 3 – Suspicious activity requiring further investigation.

**Why this severity was assigned:**

The alert involved a successful authentication from an unfamiliar location using a previously unseen protocol.

Although the activity deviated from the established baseline, the investigation didn't identify enough evidence to confirm account compromise.

---

## Response Actions

- Escalated the investigation.
- Documented the deviation from the established baseline.
- Documented the KQL findings.
- Recommended additional review by the identity and security teams.

---

## Justification

The sign-in event clearly deviated from the user's normal behavior.

However, the absence of suspicious audit events prevented the investigation from confirming malicious activity.

Additional investigation was required to determine whether the authentication represented legitimate activity or unauthorized access.
