# CLD-0103: Internal Port Scan Investigation

## Original Ticket

**Short Description:**

Internal port scan detected from 203.0.113.44

**Description:**

Rule 'TCP port sweep' fired.

Host 203.0.113.44 (LDN-SCAN-01) probed 4,000+ TCP ports across 180 internal hosts between 22:10 and 02:5x UTC.

Traffic pattern: sequential SYN probes with no data transfer.

No other alerts originated from this source.

---

## Investigation Strategy

Before beginning the investigation, I considered two possible explanations.

**Malicious hypothesis:**

An attacker had compromised an internal host and was performing reconnaissance to identify open ports and map the internal network.

**Benign hypothesis:**

The activity was generated by an authorized vulnerability scanner performing a scheduled scan.

---

## Evidence Reviewed

The first step was reviewing the shift handover documentation.

The handover identified **LDN-SCAN-01 (203.0.113.44)** as Cloudora's authorized vulnerability scanner.

The documented scan window was **Monday from 22:00 UTC through Tuesday at 03:00 UTC**.

I then compared the alert against the handover details.

The source IP address, host name, and time window matched the documented maintenance activity.

I also reviewed the alert details and confirmed that the traffic consisted of **sequential SYN probes** without any data transfer or follow-up alerts.

---

## Investigation Process

My first objective was determining whether the activity represented an internal reconnaissance attempt.

Port scanning is a common post-compromise technique because attackers frequently map a network before moving laterally.

However, the activity matched the characteristics of an authorized vulnerability scan.

The source IP matched the organization's documented scanner.

The activity occurred entirely within the approved maintenance window.

The traffic pattern also supported the benign explanation because the scan consisted exclusively of sequential SYN probes without any evidence of data movement.

No additional alerts were generated from the source, which further supported the scheduled-scan explanation.

At that point, I ruled out the malicious hypothesis.

---

## Verdict

**False Positive**

The alert was generated by an authorized vulnerability scan.

---

## Severity

### Informational — Expected and authorized activity.

**Why this severity was assigned:**

The rule originally classified the activity as **Medium severity**.

After reviewing the handover documentation and validating the traffic pattern, the alert was reclassified as **Informational** because the activity was expected and fully documented.

---

## Response Actions

- Closed the alert.
- Referenced the approved scan schedule.
- Recommended creating a rule exception for approved scanning activity during the documented maintenance window.

---

## Justification

The source, timing, traffic pattern, and host identity all matched the documented vulnerability scan.

No evidence suggested malicious reconnaissance or lateral movement.

---

## MITRE ATT&CK

**None.**

This activity was determined to be authorized and did not require MITRE ATT&CK mapping.
